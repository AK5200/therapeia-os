# Hooks

The Brain picks one hook_type; Copy loads that section, writes 5 cover candidates using it, and keeps the one the reader in viral.reader would stop for (specific, max 10 words, compliant).

## hook.list: Hook list (for the Brain)
for: brain | edit: learn

Strongest for reach (prefer these): felt_moment, surprising_fact, myth_vs_fact, permission, question, contrarian, curiosity_gap, pov.
Also: number_outcome, mistake, identity, study, timeline, comparison, save_bait, did_you_know, how_to, story.

## hook.guide: Which hook fits which post
for: brain | edit: learn

Pick the hook from what the post is about, not at random (examples are compliance-safe cover lines; adapt, never copy):
| hook | use when the post... | example cover |
|---|---|---|
| felt_moment | names an everyday moment (3am wake-ups, 3pm crash, wired at night) | "Why you're tired but wired at night (it's not just caffeine)" |
| permission | gives relief, less effort, a smaller routine | "You're not lazy. You might just be running on stress." |
| surprising_fact | explains how the body works across the day | "What cortisol actually does from 7AM to 11PM" |
| myth_vs_fact | corrects beliefs about an ingredient or habit | "Shilajit: 4 myths and 3 facts" |
| comparison | separates two things people confuse | "Tired vs. wired: how to tell the difference" |
| number_outcome | is a list of habits, signs or swaps | "7 tiny habits for steadier energy, ranked by effort" |
| mistake | stops a common habit that backfires | "Stop doing this in the hour before bed" |
| curiosity_gap | reveals the real reason behind a pattern | "The 2PM crash isn't a willpower problem" |
| study | explains one approved study | "Ashwagandha: what the study actually tested (and what it didn't)" |
| how_to / save_bait | gives a protocol to keep | "A 10-minute wind-down you can actually stick to" |
| identity / pov | speaks to one specific person | "Your focus isn't broken. Your mornings might be." |
| question | answers what people actually search | "Why do I wake up at 3am?" |
Covers that name the reader's moment, a myth or a surprising body fact beat covers that name a topic. Validation never diagnoses ("Feeling wired at night?" yes; "You have anxiety" never).

## hook.number_outcome: Number + specific outcome
for: copy | edit: learn

"5 things that quietly wreck your sleep". The number sets a finish line; each item must be specific and surprising enough to swipe for.
Examples: "7 tiny habits for steadier energy, ranked by effort" · "5 signs your nervous system is stuck in 'on'".

## hook.contrarian: Contrarian
for: copy | edit: learn

"More sleep won't fix your 3pm slump. This might." Challenge a common belief, then deliver. Must be true and fair.

## hook.myth_vs_fact: Myth callout
for: copy | edit: learn

Open with the belief in the reader's own words ("Ashwagandha knocks you out"). Correct it clearly and confidently with one plain reason. Friendly, never mocking.
Examples: "Shilajit: 4 myths and 3 facts" · "Does ashwagandha make you sleepy all day?".

## hook.curiosity_gap: Curiosity gap
for: copy | edit: learn

"It's not your mattress..." / "The 3pm crash starts at breakfast". Open a gap about the reader's own life; slide 2 or 3 closes it with a real answer.
Examples: "The 2PM crash isn't a willpower problem".

## hook.mistake: Mistake / fear of loss
for: copy | edit: learn

"Stop taking your gummies like this". Confident, never scary or shaming.
Examples: "Stop doing this in the hour before bed".

## hook.identity: Identity
for: copy | edit: learn

"If you're tired all day and wide awake at 11pm, this is for you". Speaks to a specific person through their moment, never a condition or diagnosis.
Examples: "Your focus isn't broken. Your mornings might be." · "Send this to the friend who can't switch off".

## hook.study: Study
for: copy | edit: learn

"Researchers gave stressed adults ashwagandha for 8 weeks. Here's what happened..." Only with a research fact in the brief; the cover tells the setup, the finding comes on a later slide in its approved slide wording.

## hook.timeline: Timeline
for: copy | edit: learn

"What 30 days of a wind-down routine looks like". Routine milestones, never promised results.

## hook.comparison: This or that
for: copy | edit: learn

Open with two named options ("Root powder or standardized extract?"). Compare fairly on 2 to 4 points people care about. End with who each suits. Never attack another brand.
Examples: "Tired vs. wired: how to tell the difference".

## hook.save_bait: Save bait (legitimate)
for: copy | edit: learn

"Save this before your next supplement order". Only when the post really is reference material.

## hook.question: Question
for: copy | edit: learn

The exact question the reader would type into Google or ask a friend ("Why do I wake up at 3am?", "Gummies or capsules: which fits my day?"). Max 8 words. Slide 2 gives the short answer, then the slides explain.

## hook.did_you_know: Did you know
for: copy | edit: learn

One surprising but true fact from the brief or from basic body knowledge, about the reader ("Your stress hormone is supposed to peak at 8am, not 11pm"). Slide 2 explains why it matters to them. Never invent facts.

## hook.pov: POV
for: copy | edit: learn

A specific, relatable moment ("POV: it's 3pm and your focus left the chat"). Real and a little funny, never dramatic. Then one useful takeaway.

## hook.how_to: How-to
for: copy | edit: learn

Cover promises the outcome in plain words ("How to fit ashwagandha into your evening"). One clear step per slide, starting with a verb.

## hook.story: Story
for: copy | edit: learn

Open with a short real moment (the founder, a ritual, a tradition) and build to the lesson.

## hook.weak: Weak hooks to avoid
for: copy, critic | edit: learn

Vague topic labels ("Tips for wellness", "All about ashwagandha"), questions nobody asks, hype ("You won't believe..."), and promises the slides don't keep.

## hook.felt_moment: The reader's moment
for: copy | edit: learn

Name a moment the reader lives every week, so exactly they feel seen: "If you wake up at 3am and can't fall back asleep..." / "Wired at 11pm, exhausted at 3pm?". The body line hints that there is a reason ("it may not be what you think..."). The best default hook for awareness.
Examples: "Why you're tired but wired at night (it's not just caffeine)" · "The 3AM wake-up, explained in 6 slides" · "Things nobody tells you about stress and sleep".

## hook.surprising_fact: Surprising fact about the reader
for: copy | edit: learn

A true, checkable fact that changes how they see their day: "Your body makes most of its stress hormone in the first hour after you wake". Only facts from the brief or plain, widely accepted physiology; no invented numbers.
Examples: "What cortisol actually does from 7AM to 11PM" · "Your body builds sleep pressure from the moment you wake".

## hook.permission: Permission
for: copy | edit: learn

Give relief: "Your nervous system doesn't need a 2-hour morning routine" / "You don't have to quit coffee". Then show the small version that works. Relief is shared because it helps someone you know.
Examples: "You're not lazy. You might just be running on stress." · "A 10-minute wind-down you can actually stick to" · "Your evening doesn't need 12 steps".
