# Hooks

The Brain picks one hook_type; Copy loads that section, writes 5 cover candidates using it, and keeps the strongest (specific, max 8 words, a clear payoff, compliant).

## hook.list: Hook list (for the Brain)
for: brain | edit: learn

number_outcome, contrarian, myth_vs_fact, curiosity_gap, mistake, identity, study, timeline, comparison, save_bait, question, did_you_know, pov, how_to, story

## hook.number_outcome: Number + specific outcome
for: copy | edit: learn

"5 evening habits for a calmer night". The number sets a finish line; the outcome must be specific.

## hook.contrarian: Contrarian
for: copy | edit: learn

"More sleep won't fix your 3pm slump. This might." Challenge a common belief, then deliver. Must be true and fair.

## hook.myth_vs_fact: Myth callout
for: copy | edit: learn

Open with the belief in the reader's own words ("Ashwagandha knocks you out"). Correct it calmly with one plain reason. Friendly, never mocking.

## hook.curiosity_gap: Curiosity gap
for: copy | edit: learn

"The label detail most people miss". Open a gap the slides close on slide 2 or 3. The payoff must be real.

## hook.mistake: Mistake / fear of loss
for: copy | edit: learn

"Stop taking your gummies like this". Calm, never scary or shaming.

## hook.identity: Identity
for: copy | edit: learn

"If you're 30+ and always tired but wired, read this". Speaks to a specific person. Never a condition or diagnosis.

## hook.study: Study
for: copy | edit: learn

"An 8-week study on stress, simply explained". Only when the study is in the brief; otherwise use did_you_know.

## hook.timeline: Timeline
for: copy | edit: learn

"What 30 days of a wind-down routine looks like". Routine milestones, never promised results.

## hook.comparison: This or that
for: copy | edit: learn

Open with two named options ("Root powder or standardized extract?"). Compare fairly on 2 to 4 points people care about. End with who each suits. Never attack another brand.

## hook.save_bait: Save bait (legitimate)
for: copy | edit: learn

"Save this before your next supplement order". Only when the post really is reference material.

## hook.question: Question
for: copy | edit: learn

The exact question a curious person would type into Google or ask a friend ("Will ashwagandha make me sleepy?"). Max 7 words. The next slides answer it directly, short answer first.

## hook.did_you_know: Did you know
for: copy | edit: learn

Open with one surprising but true fact from the brief, stated plainly. Slide 2 explains why it matters to the reader. Never invent facts.

## hook.pov: POV
for: copy | edit: learn

A specific, relatable moment ("POV: it's 3pm and your focus left the chat"). Real and a little funny, never dramatic. Then one useful takeaway.

## hook.how_to: How-to
for: copy | edit: learn

Cover promises the outcome in plain words ("How to fit ashwagandha into your evening"). One clear step per slide, starting with a verb.

## hook.story: Story
for: copy | edit: learn

Open with a short real moment (the founder, a ritual, a tradition) and build to the lesson.

## hook.weak: Weak hooks to avoid
for: copy, critic | edit: learn

Vague topic labels ("Tips for wellness", "All about ashwagandha"), questions nobody asks, hype ("You won't believe..."), and promises the slides don't keep.
