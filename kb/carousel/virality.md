# Virality: how our reader decides

Distilled from Instagram's own guidance (Mosseri 2024-2026: sends per reach is a top signal; skipped carousels are re-shown from slide 2), sharing research (Berger and Milkman 2012; Scholz, Baek and Falk 2023), the face effect (Bakhshi et al. 2014, 1.1M Instagram photos: faces got 38% more likes) and a teardown of top wellness carousels (Nov 2026). Read it as taste, not as a checklist.

## viral.reader: How our reader decides
for: brain, copy, critic, art | edit: learn

OUR READER: a US adult, 25 to 45, busy, often tired but wired, sleeping badly, stressed by 3pm. Has never heard of ashwagandha or shilajit, or thinks it is a TikTok trend. Scrolls fast, reads only slides, rarely opens the caption. Smart, but not into science words.

Every post wins or loses four decisions:
1. STOP (under a second, the cover). They stop for THEMSELVES: their own moment named exactly ("If you wake up at 3am every night..."), a surprising true fact about people like them, a myth they believe, permission to do less ("Your evening doesn't need a 12-step routine"), or a real face looking at them. They scroll past topics ("All about ashwagandha"), brand talk and vague wellness words.
2. SWIPE (every slide). Each slide answers the one before and opens the next. End slides on a pull: "Here's why...", "Timing is the clue.", "The fix is smaller than you think...". Explain the body in plain words, like a smart friend at the kitchen table (what cortisol does across the day, why the 3pm dip happens). Specific beats general; one idea per slide; numbered progress for lists.
3. SEND (the strongest signal). People send what helps someone they know, what makes them look smart, and what gives a jolt of surprise, relief or "so THAT's why". Name who it is for ("for the friend who's always wired at night"). Sadness and fear do not travel; calm surprise and relief do.
4. SAVE. A concrete list, routine or checklist they can use tonight or tomorrow morning, with specifics (times, amounts of time, simple swaps), never "be mindful".

THE SAUCE IS ON THE SLIDES. Every slide must teach something on its own. The caption is a bonus for the curious (sources, the study note, extra detail); a slide that only says "see caption" is a wasted slide.
NEVER: abstract topics (how to read research, our brand values, label jargon) as the main idea; a cover that names the topic instead of the reader's moment; slides with one vague line; hype, fear or shame.

## viral.arc: The story every carousel tells
for: brain, copy, critic | edit: learn

1. Cover: the reader's moment or the surprising fact, plus an open-loop line ("Here's what's actually going on...").
2. Slide 2 (works alone, Instagram may start here): name it and validate it ("You're not imagining it. It's a pattern.").
3. Why it happens: the mechanism in plain words, 1 or 2 slides.
4. What helps: specific, doable steps or swaps, one per slide (this is where the ingredient fits in, as one part of the answer, never the hero in awareness posts).
5. Payoff: the screenshot slide, a short checklist or the one-line takeaway.
6. CTA: send to someone specific, or save for a specific moment.
Archetypes change the order (a myth_fact post opens with the myth), never the pull from slide to slide.
